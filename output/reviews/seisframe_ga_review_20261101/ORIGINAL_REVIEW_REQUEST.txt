1. Search architecture and scope

2. Chromosome and design variables

Genes select discrete member sections and concrete grades.

Section genes correspond to tied groups of members. Beam genes select permitted width–depth pairs; column genes select permitted square sections.

For the saved six-story perimeter-frame pilot, the claimed chromosome has eight section genes—four member categories in each of two three-story bands—and three concrete-grade genes, for eleven genes total.

Verify this for that specific configuration. Do not assume eleven genes applies to every case.

Concrete grades are assigned by family, with changes potentially affecting multiple bands or systems.

Longitudinal bars, hoops/stirrups, and slab reinforcement are selected by inner engineering routines rather than directly mutated as chromosome entries.

3. Reinforcement ceiling

The practical beam ceiling is applied per relevant face as As/(bw·d), using the appropriate effective depth.

The base configuration reportedly defaults to 0.015, while recent pilot configurations explicitly request 0.0175.

Verify that the authorized maximum, inner cage selector, jump estimator, evaluator, saved configuration, and resumed runs apply this consistently. Check for alternate paths or cached evidence that could bypass or misinterpret it.

4. Initialization and parent selection

The default initializer evaluates the seed and random local neighbors.

The recent pilot plan specifies population size four and four offspring per generation.

Parents are selected uniformly from the surviving population. Failure severity affects mutation selection, not parent selection.
