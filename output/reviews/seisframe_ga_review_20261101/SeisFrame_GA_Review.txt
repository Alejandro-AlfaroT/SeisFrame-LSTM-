VERDICT

The description is mostly accurate about how the GA searches, but it overstates the completeness of the acceptance safeguards. The current source contains substantial corrections that the historical reports still describe as unfinished.

The review found three reproducible implementation gaps: an incomplete detailing-check inventory, inconsistent rejection of test-labelled evidence profiles, and an alternate API path around the authorized reinforcement ceiling. Separately, the slab procedure remains unreviewed. The evidence does not establish readiness for an accepted-design yield pilot.

No production code or engineering assertions were changed. No packages were installed. No GA run or structural analysis was launched. The review used source inspection, recorded results, isolated tests and small no-solve reproductions. This package preserves that completed review; it does not constitute a new validation run or authorize engineering assertions.

FINDING 1  REQUIRED DETAILING CHECKS ARE NOT FULLY INVENTORIED

Classification: Confirmed completeness-guard defect.

Trigger: A required detailing row is absent from an otherwise passing evaluation record.

Actual behavior: Code_Check_Contract.expected_inventory requires at least one detailing row per group, plus selected exact checks. It does not independently require all minimum-steel, maximum-ratio, spacing, longitudinal-bar and hoop checks that the producer emits. For beams, clear-span and column-projection rows can satisfy the broad detailing pattern.

Small reproduction: An otherwise passing synthetic assessment with a failing detailing / beam.minimum_steel / <beam_gid>/top row is check_failed. Omitting that row changes the assessment to accepted. The reproduction invokes the assessment boundary only; it performs no structural solve.

Expected behavior: Every applicable detailing identity, including face and location variants, must be present. Missing required evidence must block acceptance.

Practical consequence: The public acceptance boundary cannot substantiate a complete detailing inventory. Comparability safeguards downstream inherit this incomplete inventory.

Limit: No current production emitter was found actually dropping this row. This is a verified guard weakness, not evidence that a recorded physical design was falsely accepted.

Required correction: Enumerate the applicable detailing identities and add omission tests for each required row, rather than testing only inventories produced from the contract's own list.

Sources: Design/Code_Check_Contract.py:224-243, 451-457; Design/Grouped_Record.py:684-719; Design/SMRF_Detailing.py:199-210, 243-285, 296-364.

FINDING 2  THE V1 LOADER CAN ACCEPT EXPLICIT TEST FIXTURE ASSERTIONS

Classification: Confirmed evidence-profile guard inconsistency.

No file was saved and no assertions were activated. test_design_evidence_profile.py:487 currently expects this behavior as an accepted control.

Practical consequence: An intended engineering-evidence safeguard is incomplete.

Its case, profile and floor identifiers are artificial, its two-way assessment is false, and local-steel assessment is omitted. This is a production-loader guard bypass requiring explicit relabelling, not automatic structural acceptance.

Required correction: Apply the existing fixture-text prohibition to all non-test schemas and change the existing control test to require rejection.

FINDING 3  THE AUTHORIZED REINFORCEMENT CEILING IS NOT UNIVERSAL

Classification: Confirmed alternate-entry-point inconsistency.

Trigger: A caller supplies a mutable DesignConfig with rho_beam_practical_max above 0.0175 directly to the legacy search or engineering evaluator/selector path.

Actual behavior: The runner's with_beam_practical_ratio helper and GenerationRun reject a higher ceiling. Genetic_Search.search validates its search policy but passes the engineering configuration through; the inner selector trusts that configuration and applies the minimum of its code cap and configured practical cap, without independently applying the authorized 0.0175 limit.

Small reproduction: Pure cage pricing with a supplied ceiling of 0.020 admitted a two-layer cage with seven #9 bars per face and actual As/(bw*d)=0.019568690095846646. The same cage was rejected at 0.0175 with steel_ratio_practical. OpenSees analyze and eigen were guarded; no search or analysis ran.

Expected behavior: Shared validation at engineering entry points enforces the authorized maximum.

Practical consequence: Alternate callers can violate the project decision. No evidence was found that the protected pilot configuration exceeded 0.0175.

Ratio wording qualification: The selector checks max(As_top,As_bottom)/(bw*min(d_positive,d_negative)), rather than pairing each face's area and depth independently. It is conservative and equivalent for equal-face reinforcement, as in the grouped symmetric pilot. It does not let a face exceed the supplied ceiling. Jump pricing shares the same rule.

Required correction: Centralize validation. Do not increase the ceiling.

Sources: Design/Config.py:154, 604-629, 665-680; Design/Genetic_Search.py:306-367; Design/Genetic_Generation.py:450-453; Design/Group_Selection.py:343-354; tools/genetic_design_search/run.py:219-221.

MISSING ENGINEERING EVIDENCE AND CURRENT CORRECTIONS

Both checked-in evidence examples remain unreviewed. The v2 example loads as inactive_unreviewed, asserted false, with no method-hash mismatch. All seven engineering dispositions remain open: floor idealization, supports and faces, stiffness sensitivity, two-way shear path, local slab-column steel, independent reference, and tolerance. There is no reviewed procedure in the runner's evidence_profiles directory.

The procedure infrastructure checks method identity, domain and candidate-specific floor evidence. It does not establish engineering adequacy. ACI 318-19 section 8.4.4.1.1 requires two-way shear evaluation near columns, concentrated loads and reaction areas; the proposed beam-supported interpretation still needs its evidence-backed disposition. Mesh agreement alone cannot supply that judgment.

The working tree is ahead of the historical closure and follow-up reports in the following ways:

- Two-way shear and local slab-column steel are independent required slab-completion items. Each needs its own assessment and valid premise; review relabelling cannot silently bypass them.
- Fire is an explicit scope exclusion. It is not checked or passed, and a bogus pass/fail/review row for the exclusion is rejected.
- Hook logic records support-face and joint-face embedments. SCWB uses development at either datum; beam sizing uses both; hinges and saved strengths retain their support-face basis. These consumers remain distinct.
- Corner-hook location and confinement factors depend on the declared geometry.
- Roof SCWB applicability is checked per connection using discontinuity above the connection and the column's own Ag*f'c/10 limit, with recorded axial-load evidence. Missing evidence does not create an exemption.
- Procedure profiles bind method files, applicability domain, numerical protocol, dispositions and per-floor checks. Inactive or changed methods do not authorize assertions.

ACCEPTANCE QUALIFICATION AND PERFORMANCE

Accepted remains a scoped candidate classification. The contract intentionally permits listed regularity/story-strength/torsion review dependencies. Its candidate scope explicitly excludes diaphragm and foundation design. Grouped qualification separately retains unresolved diaphragm.design_and_collectors and other applicable strength-model, constructability, model-consistency and transition reviews. Completing the slab procedure alone therefore does not establish fully qualified grouped designs.

These are visible scope limitations, not evidence that the software accidentally treats every unknown as a pass. They must remain visible in reporting and in the definition of the capstone finish line. Nonlinear performance is separately not_requested; ordinary candidate evaluation does not perform earthquake time-history validation.

Sources: Design/Code_Check_Contract.py:65-97, 326-330, 475-486, 519-524; Design/Grouped_Record.py:875-925, 981-991, 1206.

ENGINEERING GUIDED OPERATORS

| Route | Supported trigger and action |
| H1 | Authenticated applicable SCWB failure with valid joint/axis/sign and connected-column inventory: increase connected column section ties or their concrete-family grade. |
| H2 | Beam flexure or capacity shear: width, depth or grade. Column P-M or capacity shear: section or grade. Column growth is withheld under the authenticated minimum-ratio cage and column-own probable-shear mechanism. Capacity-shear routing requires the transverse-selection outcome. |
| H3 | Pure width blockage: width. Threading: evidenced blocking cores or transitions. Lane-limited strength: beam dimensions and relevant cores. A mixed threading-plus-flexural deficit may also use grade. Pure geometric clearance does not receive a grade remedy. |
| H4 | Joint shear: core size or grade. Through-bar depth and hook embedment: core size. Through-bar and terminating-bar dependencies are distinguished. |
| H5 | Relevant SFRS story columns and direction-matching beam depths at floors story-1 and story. |
| R1 | Uniform mutable gene, then uniform permitted local neighbor. Grade moves use nearest lower/higher choices; section neighbors follow the declared dimensional rule. |

Authentication binds diagnostics to the evaluated parent's realized identity and evaluation basis. Routing requires failed status, applicability, valid evidence and the reason's dependencies; numeric contradiction causes abstention. Threading records also authenticate the installed cage and blocking information. These are software provenance checks, not independent proof of engineering assumptions.

UNSUPPORTED ROUTES AND CONSERVATIVE ABSTENTIONS

- Analysis-shear components have no supported mutation reason. A strength failure does not automatically become a capacity-shear diagnosis.
- Production slab-mat overlap leaves blocked_dimension=None and diagnosed_dimension=False. H3 therefore abstains, despite the nominal route existing.
- The nominal beam_row_depth operator lacks a production diagnostic source when evaluation stops before normal code-check rows; that stopped design is not an ordinary selection parent.
- Unlisted slab, selected-hoop and other final checks remain unsupported rather than being converted into invented strengthening instructions.
- Verified cycle resolution does not establish ordinary selector convergence for geometry-routing dependencies. That conservative choice is separate from numerical completion under the contract.
- Exhausted catalogs and unsupported transition look-ahead may also yield no legal move. Missing or invalid evidence is reported separately.

DEMAND SIZED JUMPS AND PREFERENCE

The estimator covers authenticated beam_flexure, beam_lane_limited_strength and beam_threading_constrained_strength shortfalls. Required capacity is Mu/target_DCR. The 0.85 target is a search setting, not a multiplier on dimensions or reinforcement and not an engineering acceptance threshold.

Pricing uses final evaluated slab/floor context, actual bar arrangement and effective depths, paired demands, admissibility, threading and the configured ratio ceiling. Surrounding cages and parent demands remain frozen. Parent-baseline agreement within the implemented 2 percent tolerance is required before returning a screened target.

The scan is bounded rather than an exhaustive cage optimization: it scans descending steel and uses a 75 percent cutoff after the first admissible threading cage. Repair is applied before pricing, and the requested allele must survive repair. Target-reaching options are distinguished from useful partial improvements. Unpriced alternatives remain unscreened and cannot acquire screened-preference status merely because the budget expired; normal local routes can still propose a candidate for full evaluation.

A concrete-family gene changes all groups in that family, but its screen prices only beam units with authenticated eligible flexural shortfalls. The gene estimate is the worst among those screened units, not a complete family-wide reevaluation. Individual-unit baselines and predicted improvements prevent one band's improvement from being credited to another failure.

The eight-pricing count includes the parent baseline for size screening. Each grade option consumes pricing work for its screened units. The fifteen-second observation deadline is capped by remaining run time but checked between cages, options and units; an operation already in progress can overrun it. Every new input proposed after screening still receives the full evaluator.

Severity weighting collapses repeated signs/faces/groups into failure keys and uses the worst authenticated utilization, rather than summing duplicate rows. Preferred proposals need their own affected unit's useful predicted gain and a threading cage. Exhausted preferred candidates leave that branch. Normal guidance, including clearance and column coordination, remains available when a beam-only change cannot reach the target. A preferred draw can be rejected, repaired to a no-op, duplicated or cached, so 0.75*0.80=0.60 does not predict the share of completed evaluations.

Sources: Design/Genetic_Jumps.py:95-151, 158-262, 288-450, 452-539, 576-578; Design/Genetic_Operators.py:575-601, 661-723, 785-855, 967-1007; Design/Genetic_Generation.py:943-950, 1118-1161, 1249-1357.

CANDIDATE EVALUATION AND EVIDENCE CONSISTENCY

1. Install the candidate, enforce concrete families, size the slab, derive floor descriptions and obtain floor transfer/action evidence. Reject an invalid starting beam-row arrangement before ordinary strength evaluation rather than silently certifying it.
2. Recompute the elastic period, torsional assessment, explicit signed strength combinations, member actions and gravity-frame displacement compatibility.
3. Reselect longitudinal cages, coordinate beam-column threading, recompute capacity design and hoops, and stop on convergence, a verified cycle resolution or the applicable bound.
4. Reprice slab reinforcement on selected cages and repeat coordination if the slab layout changes. Rebuild capacity/joint evidence if the final slab eligibility signature changes.
5. Compute final strength, drift, stability, regularity and material quantities; derive the expected inventory; apply the contract; record final cages, slab identities and evidence currency.

Sources: Design/Grouped_Design.py:354-448, 1007-1096, 1273-1397, 1403-1520.

Caching is specific rather than blanket reuse. Floor transfer reuse requires matching sections, mesh, slab, loading/patterns, geometry and slab-input digest. Slab-action cache keys include floor signature, inputs, refinement, assertions and procedure identity/status; current floor instances are checked and combined evidence replayed. Gravity-only frame actions are cached within one candidate. Section-strength caches are local to fixed group/cage/slab contexts and distinguish flange, support and anchorage conditions.

Sources: Design/SMRF_Floor_Transfer.py:318-330; Design/Grouped_Design.py:239-284; Design/Perimeter_Systems.py:130-179; Design/Group_Checks.py:250-275, 325-337.

Verified cycle resolution is not ordinary convergence. On a proven repeated state, the resolver tries at most four distinct cycle states. Adoption requires verification of the installed cages against selection rules, rebuilt capacity evidence, fixed hoops and accepted capacity checks. The ordinary selection flags remain false; only a bound resolution for the final identity can satisfy the alternate completion rule. The positive unit-test path is software evidence, not a demonstrated production cycle solution; one adoption test forces capacity acceptance to exercise control flow.

Sources: Design/Grouped_Design.py:947-1096; Design/Group_Selection.py:1670-1738; Design/Code_Check_Contract.py:76-84 and numerical_completion; tests/test_cycle_resolution.py:438-448.

Ordinary GA evaluation uses cracked elastic design members with P-Delta effects. It is not nonlinear earthquake time-history validation, regardless of an analysis-profile name. Sources: Design/Design_Driver.py:65, 553-610; Design/SMRF_Elastic.py:46-92; Design/Grouped_Design.py:1502.
