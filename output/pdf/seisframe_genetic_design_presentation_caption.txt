Presentation overview of the SeisFrame mutation-driven genetic algorithm for reinforced-concrete frame design. Within each design case, the building layout and loading remain fixed while the search varies member section dimensions and concrete grades. The three schematic options illustrate lower-story design choices, not measured pilot results: A uses 24 by 24 in columns, 18 by 24 in beams and 5 ksi concrete; B increases the column dimensions to 30 by 30 in; C instead increases beam depth to 30 in and concrete strength to 6 ksi. These illustrative values do not imply that any of the three designs passes the engineering checks. Amber emphasizes altered member groups; purple marks the higher concrete grade. Section insets are drawn at a common scale, while frame member line weights are exaggerated for clarity. Reinforcement is selected and checked by the evaluator rather than freely mutated as a bar-count gene. Evaluation identifies governing weaknesses. Promising candidates are retained and engineering-guided mutations propose changes, followed by full re-evaluation; random exploration is also retained. The lower return path explicitly depicts selection of promising designs, mutation, updated candidates in the next generation and repeated testing. Selected icons indicate retention, not qualification or demonstrated results for options A, B or C. The strong-column / weak-beam tile explicitly identifies that engineering objective. The five visual tiles describe change mechanisms and engineering targets, not a one-to-one enumeration of named software operators. Selection is separate from code acceptance and final qualification. The final record represents the intended verified output, not a claim about current software validation or demonstrated run yield.
