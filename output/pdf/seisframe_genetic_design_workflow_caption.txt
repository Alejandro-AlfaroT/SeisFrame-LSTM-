Figure X. Automated reinforced-concrete frame design using an engineering-guided genetic search. (a) A seed design and random local neighbors initialize a population encoded by grouped member dimensions and concrete grades. Each candidate undergoes structural analysis, reinforcement selection and code-check assessment. Eligible parent and offspring records are ranked by evidence/feasibility tiers, comparable violation measures and design diversity at generation boundaries. Uniformly sampled parents produce a batch of guided or random offspring mutations, evaluated serially; catalog/coupling repair and duplicate filtering precede evaluation of a new child. (b) Five diagnostic-driven operator families address strength, constructability, joints and drift; severe beam-flexure deficits can favor screened demand-sized changes. Estimates rank proposals and do not replace full evaluation. (c) Code-accepted designs are archived separately from independent qualification and downstream analysis. Incomplete or unresolved outcomes retain their evidence status. SCWB denotes strong-column/weak-beam; μ and λ denote working-population and offspring counts, respectively.

Implementation note (not part of the paper caption): The figure describes the inspected SeisFrame v3_7 mutation-driven controller, which has no crossover or learned operator weights. Dashed stages are separate downstream qualification activities, not a claim that those gates have passed. Current slab/SCWB validation and cage-coordination work remains open. Guided probabilities, population sizes, budgets and reinforcement limits are study settings and are intentionally not hard-coded into this process schematic.
